# Validated Vertical Slice — Grammar

**Status:** VALIDATED  
**Reference competency:** `GRAM.PRESENT_SIMPLE.THIRD_PERSON`

## Learner outcome

The learner recognizes and independently produces appropriate Present Simple third-person singular forms, maintains them in a new familiar context, and can later retrieve them without an explicit grammar cue.

## Prerequisites and modalities

Prerequisites include subject recognition, singular/plural distinction, Present Simple affirmative meaning, and base verb access. Evidence is separated for recognition, controlled writing, independent writing, and speaking; pronunciation realization is a separate competency.

## Slice

1. Context contrasts `I work / my sister works` and similar examples.
2. Notice/compare activity elicits the pattern without counting as mastery.
3. Guided rule reveal covers semantic subject recognition, `-s/-es`, `-y`, and `have -> has` progressively.
4. Worked reasoning identifies subject, person/number, tense/function, then form.
5. Example fading removes steps.
6. Guided practice uses a support ladder and records support cost.
7. Controlled practice mixes recognition, construction, mixed subjects, error correction, and overgeneralization distractors.
8. Independent production describes another person's routine without a grammar reminder.
9. Observations identify correct and missing markings, spelling patterns, and self-correction.
10. Feedback asks for self-correction before escalating from general cue to targeted cue, rule reminder, then worked correction.
11. Transfer uses a different person/topic and later spontaneous speaking.
12. Delayed mixed retrieval verifies retention without labeling the target.

## Interpretation

`3/5 = 60%` is rejected. Evidence distinguishes basic marking, irregular/spelling behavior, independence, consistency, modality, and support. Correct after a strong hint is positive but low-independence evidence. Immediate end-of-lesson success may reach familiarity, not mastery.

Repeated missing marking across independent writing, speaking, and delayed review may confirm an issue. Minimal probes distinguish concept knowledge from automatic retrieval/monitoring. Recovery then targets the cause—often rapid subject classification and production automaticity—rather than reteaching the whole rule. Resolution requires a new-family retest and later verification.
